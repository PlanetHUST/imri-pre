LISA Detectability of AGN-Disk EMRIs and IMRIs

From astrophysical simulations to waveform SNR, selection functions, and measurable gas signatures

A research-level workflow for converting AGN-disk population and hydrodynamic simulations into quantitative predictions for LISA. The central separation is between source formation, gravitational-wave detection, and identification of the astrophysical environment.

1. Physical picture

An AGN-disk simulation can tell us how frequently stellar-mass black holes, hierarchically grown black holes, or intermediate-mass black holes migrate toward a central massive black hole. That intrinsic astrophysical rate is not yet a LISA prediction. A gravitational-wave selection function must be applied to determine which systems are observable.

2. What has the AGN simulation produced: EMRI or IMRI?

Let the central massive black hole have mass \(M_\bullet\) and the secondary black hole mass \(\mu\). Define

\[ q=\frac{\mu}{M_\bullet}. \]

There is no mathematically sharp boundary separating EMRIs and IMRIs, but the mass ratio is the relevant physical variable because it determines which gravitational-wave approximation is appropriate.

Example Mass ratio Approximate regime
\(M_\bullet=10^6M_\odot,\ \mu=10M_\odot\) \(q=10^{-5}\) EMRI-like
\(M_\bullet=10^6M_\odot,\ \mu=10^2M_\odot\) \(q=10^{-4}\) EMRI / transition regime
\(M_\bullet=10^6M_\odot,\ \mu=10^3M_\odot\) \(q=10^{-3}\) IMRI-like
\(M_\bullet=10^6M_\odot,\ \mu=10^4M_\odot\) \(q=10^{-2}\) Strongly intermediate mass ratio
Waveform implication. A waveform model should be selected from the actual values of \(q,a_\bullet,e,\iota\), and the required phase accuracy—not simply from the label “EMRI” or “IMRI.”

3. Minimum output required from the astrophysical simulation

Ideally, each simulated inspiral should produce a source record

\[ \lambda_i= \{ M_\bullet,\, \mu,\, a_\bullet,\, z,\, e,\, p,\, \iota_{\rm Kerr},\, t_{\rm plunge},\, w_i,\, {\cal D}_{\rm AGN} \}. \]
Quantity Physical meaning Why LISA needs it
\(M_\bullet\) SMBH mass Sets relativistic orbital frequencies
\(\mu\) Secondary BH mass Controls mass ratio and GW amplitude
\(a_\bullet\) Dimensionless SMBH spin Changes separatrix, frequencies and waveform structure
\(z\) Cosmological redshift Sets detector-frame masses and luminosity distance
\(e\) Eccentricity at a defined reference point Determines harmonic structure
\(p\) Semilatus rectum or equivalent radial variable Defines initial relativistic orbit
\(\iota_{\rm Kerr}\) Inclination to SMBH equatorial plane Determines generic Kerr dynamics
\(t_{\rm plunge}\) Time remaining before plunge Determines which portion of the waveform LISA observes
\(w_i\) Monte-Carlo or astrophysical rate weight Needed for population integration

Useful AGN quantities

\[ {\cal D}_{\rm AGN} = \left\{ \Sigma(r),\, H/r,\, c_s,\, \alpha_{\rm visc},\, \dot M,\, T_{\rm gas}(r,t),\, \dot M_2,\ldots \right\}. \]
The most valuable direct output for connecting a hydrodynamic simulation to a gravitational-wave calculation is often \[ \boxed{ T_{\rm gas}(r,t)=\dot L_{\rm gas}. } \] It permits the gas dynamics to enter directly into the orbital evolution.

4. Does the source enter the LISA band?

A quick first check is obtained using the Schwarzschild innermost stable circular orbit. For a circular orbit around a nonspinning black hole,

\[ r_{\rm ISCO}=6\frac{GM_\bullet}{c^2}. \]

The dominant \(m=2\) gravitational-wave frequency near ISCO is approximately

\[ \boxed{ f_{\rm ISCO} \simeq \frac{c^3} {6^{3/2}\pi G M_{\bullet,z}} } \] \[ \boxed{ f_{\rm ISCO} \simeq 4.4\,{\rm mHz} \left( \frac{10^6M_\odot}{M_{\bullet,z}} \right). } \]

This scaling explains why massive black holes around \(10^5-10^7M_\odot\) are especially important for LISA.

This is only a screening estimate. Kerr spin, eccentricity and orbital inclination substantially modify the strong-field frequencies and termination of the inspiral.

5. Cosmological redshift and detector-frame quantities

If the simulation provides source-frame masses, the waveform observed by LISA is controlled by redshifted masses:

\[ \boxed{ M_{\bullet,z}=(1+z)M_\bullet, \qquad \mu_z=(1+z)\mu. } \]

The observed frequencies satisfy

\[ f_{\rm obs}=\frac{f_{\rm src}}{1+z}. \]

The amplitude depends on luminosity distance:

\[ h\propto \frac{1}{D_L(z)}. \]

Consequently, for otherwise identical sources,

\[ \boxed{\rho\propto D_L^{-1}.} \]
Common error: do not use comoving distance in the waveform amplitude. Use luminosity distance \(D_L\).

6. Fundamental matched-filter SNR

Suppose the detector data are

\[ d(t)=h(t;\boldsymbol{\lambda})+n(t). \]

For stationary Gaussian noise with one-sided power spectral density \(S_n(f)\), define the noise-weighted inner product

\[ (a|b) = 4\,{\rm Re} \int_0^\infty df\, \frac{\tilde a^*(f)\tilde b(f)}{S_n(f)}. \]

The optimal matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio is

\[ \boxed{ \rho^2=(h|h) = 4\int_0^\infty df\, \frac{|\tilde h(f)|^2}{S_n(f)}. } \]

The integrand

\[ \frac{|\tilde h(f)|^2}{S_n(f)} \]

makes the physical meaning transparent: waveform power contributes most strongly where the detector noise is lowest.

7. Why coherent accumulation matters

For a slowly evolving nearly monochromatic signal with amplitude \(h_0\), a schematic scaling is

\[ \rho \sim \frac{h_0}{\sqrt{S_n(f)}}\sqrt{T_{\rm obs}}. \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{\rho\propto \sqrt{T_{\rm obs}}} \]

when the assumptions behind the approximation apply.

EMRIs can accumulate very large numbers of coherent orbital cycles. A weak instantaneous signal can therefore become a high-SNR source after years of phase-coherent integration.

The condition \[ h(f)>\sqrt{S_n(f)} \] is not a valid general detection criterion for a long-lived inspiral.

8. Characteristic-strain representation

For a consistent convention, define

\[ h_c(f)=2f|\tilde h(f)| \]

and

\[ h_n(f)=\sqrt{fS_n(f)}. \]

Then

\[ \boxed{ \rho^2 = \int \left[ \frac{h_c(f)}{h_n(f)} \right]^2 d\ln f. } \]

This representation is excellent for physical intuition. It shows that SNR is an integrated noise-weighted quantity across logarithmic frequency, not simply a curve-crossing test.

Use characteristic-strain plots to understand where the source accumulates SNR, but use the full numerical inner product for final population selection.

9. LISA response and time-delay interferometry

The astrophysical calculation generates two polarizations,

\[ h_+(t),\qquad h_\times(t). \]

LISA does not measure these polarizations directly. The moving constellation maps them into time-delay interferometry channels:

\[ h_C(t)= {\cal R}_C [ h_+,h_\times; \theta,\phi,\psi,t ]. \]

For approximately noise-orthogonal \(A,E\) channels,

\[ \boxed{ \rho^2 = 4\int df \left[ \frac{|\tilde h_A(f)|^2}{S_A(f)} + \frac{|\tilde h_E(f)|^2}{S_E(f)} \right]. } \]

More generally, for correlated channels,

\[ \boxed{ \rho^2 = 4\,{\rm Re} \int df\, \tilde h_i^*(f) [S_n^{-1}(f)]_{ij} \tilde h_j(f). } \]

The LISA response depends on sky location, polarization, source orientation, frequency, and the orbital motion of the constellation.

10. Full detector response versus sky-averaged sensitivity

Method A: explicit detector response

Generate

\[ h_+,h_\times \rightarrow h_A,h_E \]

and use the corresponding TDI noise PSDs \(S_A,S_E\).

Method B: sky-averaged effective sensitivity

Use an averaged detector response and an effective strain noise curve \(S_h^{\rm eff}(f)\).

Never mix the two conventions. If a waveform has already been projected through the full LISA response, do not also use a sensitivity curve that has already been divided by an averaged response. That effectively counts the detector response twice.

11. Fast quasi-circular inspiral SNR estimate

For early population screening, define

\[ M=M_\bullet+\mu, \] \[ \eta=\frac{M_\bullet\mu}{M^2}, \]

and the chirp mass

\[ \boxed{ {\cal M} = M\eta^{3/5} = \frac{(M_\bullet\mu)^{3/5}} {(M_\bullet+\mu)^{1/5}}. } \]

Use the redshifted chirp mass

\[ {\cal M}_z=(1+z){\cal M}. \]

In the Newtonian stationary-phase approximation, schematically,

\[ |\tilde h(f)| \simeq \sqrt{\frac{5}{24}} \pi^{-2/3} \frac{{\cal M}_z^{5/6}}{D_L} f^{-7/6} {\cal F}. \]

Therefore

\[ \rho^2 \propto \frac{{\cal M}_z^{5/3}}{D_L^2} \int \frac{f^{-7/3}}{S_h(f)} \,df. \]

Roughly,

\[ \boxed{ \rho\propto \frac{{\cal M}_z^{5/6}}{D_L} } \]

when the frequency weighting is otherwise similar.

This approximation is useful for population triage. It should not be used as the final selection function for eccentric, relativistic EMRIs or IMRIs.

12. Determining the observed frequency range

Start frequency

The correct lower frequency is determined by where the particular binary is when the observation begins.

For a circular Newtonian inspiral,

\[ t_c-t = \frac{5}{256} \left( \frac{G{\cal M}_z}{c^3} \right)^{-5/3} (\pi f)^{-8/3}. \]

If the available observation time is \(T_{\rm obs}\),

\[ \boxed{ f_{\rm start} = \frac{1}{\pi} \left( \frac{5}{256T_{\rm obs}} \right)^{3/8} \left( \frac{G{\cal M}_z}{c^3} \right)^{-5/8}. } \]

Upper frequency

For a crude Schwarzschild calculation,

\[ f_{\max}\sim f_{\rm ISCO}. \]

For realistic Kerr EMRIs or IMRIs, the upper limit should instead come from the relativistic trajectory and separatrix/plunge prescription.

Save \(t_{\rm plunge}\) or the orbital state at mission start. It is often as important for SNR as the source masses themselves.

13. Eccentric and generic Kerr sources

An eccentric binary radiates at multiple harmonics. In a Newtonian picture,

\[ f_n\simeq n f_{\rm orb}. \]

A generic bound Kerr orbit has three fundamental frequencies,

\[ \Omega_r,\qquad \Omega_\theta,\qquad \Omega_\phi. \]

The waveform contains harmonic combinations

\[ \boxed{ \omega_{mkn} = m\Omega_\phi + k\Omega_\theta + n\Omega_r. } \]

The observed frequencies are

\[ f^{\rm obs}_{mkn} = \frac{\omega_{mkn}} {2\pi(1+z)}. \]

Eccentricity can move substantial power into harmonics lying in more sensitive parts of the LISA band. Consequently, circular approximations may substantially misestimate SNR for eccentric systems.

14. Choosing the waveform model

For very small mass ratios, black-hole perturbation theory and self-force methods are natural. At larger \(q\), neglected higher-order mass-ratio corrections become increasingly important.

Mass ratio Initial modeling strategy Important concern
\(q\lesssim10^{-5}\) Relativistic EMRI / self-force framework Generic Kerr coverage and self-force order
\(q\sim10^{-4}\) EMRI framework with systematic validation Higher-order mass-ratio corrections
\(q\sim10^{-3}\) Compare perturbative and IMRI/EOB descriptions Accumulated waveform bias
\(q\gtrsim10^{-2}\) Intermediate/comparable-mass methods become increasingly important Pure perturbative approximation may fail
These boundaries are schematic. The real criterion is whether waveform error, after accumulating across the observation, is small enough relative to the SNR and the scientific inference being attempted.

15. Numerical SNR calculation

After producing frequency-domain TDI signals,

\[ \tilde h_A(f_k),\qquad \tilde h_E(f_k), \]

calculate

\[ \boxed{ \rho^2 \simeq 4\sum_k \left[ \frac{|\tilde h_A(f_k)|^2}{S_A(f_k)} + \frac{|\tilde h_E(f_k)|^2}{S_E(f_k)} \right] \Delta f. } \]

For a time series of length \(T\),

\[ \Delta f=\frac{1}{T}. \] 
rho2_A = 4.0 * np.sum(
    np.abs(hA_f)**2 / Sn_A * df
)

rho2_E = 4.0 * np.sum(
    np.abs(hE_f)**2 / Sn_E * df
)

rho = np.sqrt(rho2_A + rho2_E)

The integral itself is easy. The scientifically difficult part is generating a physically consistent waveform, LISA response, PSD, observation interval, and source population.

16. What SNR counts as detectable?

There is no universal fundamental value of \(\rho\) that defines detection. The practical threshold depends on search strategy, waveform complexity, overlapping sources, foregrounds, non-Gaussian noise, and false-alarm requirements.

For population forecasting, a useful strategy is to report several thresholds, for example

\[ \boxed{ \rho_{\rm th}=15,\ 20,\ 30. } \]

A common fiducial forecast choice is \(\rho_{\rm th}=20\), with a more conservative \(\rho_{\rm th}=30\) used to test robustness.

Treat the SNR threshold as a forecast assumption, not as an immutable LISA mission specification.

17. Detection probability and selection function

Your astrophysical simulation predicts intrinsic parameters, but sky position and observer orientation are usually unknown.

Sample extrinsic parameters such as

\[ \lambda_{\rm ext} = \{\theta,\phi,\psi,\iota_{\rm LOS},\ldots\}. \]

Then define

\[ \boxed{ p_{\rm det}(\lambda_{\rm int},z) = \int d\lambda_{\rm ext}\, p(\lambda_{\rm ext}) \Theta[ \rho(\lambda)-\rho_{\rm th} ]. } \]

Numerically,

\[ p_{\rm det} \simeq \frac{1}{N_{\rm ang}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\rm ang}} \Theta(\rho_j-\rho_{\rm th}). \]

Thus an intrinsic system may have \(p_{\rm det}=0.70\) rather than being assigned a binary yes/no classification.

Recommended object to precompute

\[ \boxed{ p_{\rm det} = p_{\rm det} ( M_\bullet, \mu, a_\bullet, e, z, t_{\rm plunge}, \iota_{\rm Kerr}, \ldots ). } \]

A precomputed interpolation grid is much more efficient than running a full waveform for every event in a large AGN Monte Carlo population.

18. From intrinsic event rate to LISA-detected rate

Suppose the astrophysical model predicts a source-frame comoving rate density

\[ {\cal R}(\lambda,z). \]

For events treated as coalescences during the observing interval,

\[ \boxed{ N_{\rm det} = T_{\rm obs} \int dz\,d\lambda\, \frac{dV_c}{dz} \frac{{\cal R}(\lambda,z)}{1+z} p_{\rm det}(\lambda,z). } \]

The factor \(1/(1+z)\) converts the source-frame rate to observer-frame time.

If the simulation predicts a rate per AGN

Suppose the result is

\[ \Gamma_{\rm IMRI} ( M_\bullet, X,z ) \quad [{\rm AGN}^{-1}{\rm yr}^{-1}]. \]

Then first construct the cosmic rate:

\[ \boxed{ {\cal R}_{\rm IMRI}(z) = \int dM_\bullet\,dX\, n_{\rm AGN}(M_\bullet,X,z) \Gamma_{\rm IMRI}(M_\bullet,X,z). } \]

Only after this astrophysical population convolution should the LISA selection function be applied.

19. Merger rate is not always the same as number of systems in band

Long-lived sources can remain in the LISA band without plunging during the mission. Their instantaneous abundance is controlled by residence time.

\[ dt=\frac{df}{\dot f}. \]

Very schematically,

\[ dN \sim {\cal R}_{\rm obs} \frac{df}{\dot f_{\rm obs}}, \]

so

\[ \boxed{ \frac{dN}{df}\propto\frac{{\cal R}}{\dot f}. } \]
Ask explicitly whether your simulation predicts a formation rate, disk-capture rate, entry rate into the GW-driven regime, or final plunge rate. These can differ substantially when migration delays, traps, ejections, or long residence times are present.

20. Detecting the source versus detecting the AGN environment

Intrinsic signal strength

\[ \boxed{ \rho_{\rm opt} = \sqrt{(h_{\rm AGN}|h_{\rm AGN})}. } \]

Recovery with a vacuum template

\[ \rho_{\rm rec} = \frac{ (h_{\rm AGN}|h_{\rm vac}) }{ \sqrt{(h_{\rm vac}|h_{\rm vac})} }. \]

Define the normalized match

\[ {\cal M} = \frac{ (h_{\rm AGN}|h_{\rm vac}) }{ \sqrt{ (h_{\rm AGN}|h_{\rm AGN}) (h_{\rm vac}|h_{\rm vac}) } }. \]

Then

\[ \rho_{\rm rec} = {\cal M}\rho_{\rm opt}. \]

For an actual template search, maximize over vacuum parameters:

\[ \boxed{ {\rm FF} = \max_{\theta_{\rm vac}} \frac{ (h_{\rm AGN}|h_{\rm vac}(\theta_{\rm vac})) }{ \sqrt{ (h_{\rm AGN}|h_{\rm AGN}) (h_{\rm vac}|h_{\rm vac}) } }. } \]

This maximization matters because environmental effects can partly mimic changes in SMBH mass, spin, eccentricity or initial orbital elements.

Environmental residual

Let \(h_{\rm vac,best}\) be the best-fit vacuum waveform and define

\[ \delta h=h_{\rm AGN}-h_{\rm vac,best}. \]

A useful diagnostic is

\[ \boxed{ \rho_{\rm env}^2=(\delta h|\delta h). } \]
A source can satisfy \(\rho_{\rm opt}\gg\rho_{\rm th}\) while simultaneously having \(\rho_{\rm env}\ll1\). In that case LISA detects the binary but cannot identify its AGN environment from the waveform alone.

21. From hydrodynamic gas torque to the gravitational-wave phase

For an approximately circular coplanar system, write

\[ \boxed{ \dot L_{\rm total} = \dot L_{\rm GW} + \dot L_{\rm gas}. } \]

If the simulation measures \(T_{\rm gas}=\dot L_{\rm gas}\), then

\[ \boxed{ \dot r = \left( \frac{dL_{\rm orb}}{dr} \right)^{-1} \left[ \dot L_{\rm GW} + T_{\rm gas} \right]. } \]

The orbital phase follows

\[ \dot\phi=\Omega[r(t)], \] \[ \phi(t)=\int \Omega[r(t)]\,dt. \]

For the dominant circular quadrupole harmonic, approximately

\[ \Phi_{\rm GW}\simeq 2\phi. \]

The direct bridge between AGN hydrodynamics and LISA is therefore

\(T_{\rm gas}(r,t)\)
→
\(\dot r\)
→
\(r(t)\)
→
\(\Phi(t)\)
→
\(h(t)\)

Generic Kerr caveat

A generic relativistic orbit involves the constants

\[ E,\qquad L_z,\qquad Q. \]

An environmental model may therefore require

\[ \dot E = \dot E_{\rm GW} + \dot E_{\rm env}, \] \[ \dot L_z = \dot L_{z,\rm GW} + \dot L_{z,\rm env}, \] \[ \dot Q = \dot Q_{\rm GW} + \dot Q_{\rm env}. \]

Circular coplanar AGN-disk systems are a useful first research target because they reduce this complexity.

22. Perturbative gas-induced dephasing

Write

\[ \dot f = \dot f_{\rm vac} + \delta\dot f_{\rm gas}. \]

The accumulated phase is

\[ \Phi = \int \frac{2\pi f}{\dot f}\,df. \]

If

\[ |\delta\dot f_{\rm gas}| \ll |\dot f_{\rm vac}|, \]

then

\[ \frac{1}{\dot f} \simeq \frac{1}{\dot f_{\rm vac}} - \frac{\delta\dot f_{\rm gas}} {\dot f_{\rm vac}^2}. \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{ \delta\Phi_{\rm gas} \simeq - \int 2\pi f \frac{ \delta\dot f_{\rm gas} }{ \dot f_{\rm vac}^2 } df. } \]

This shows why even a dynamically small gas force may become observationally important after coherent accumulation over many cycles.

The condition \(|\delta\Phi|\gtrsim1\) rad is a useful physical diagnostic, but not by itself a measurement criterion. Detectability must include noise weighting and refitting of vacuum parameters.

23. Useful AGN-specific dimensionless diagnostics

Gas torque relative to GW torque

\[ \boxed{ {\cal T}(r) = \frac{T_{\rm gas}} {\dot L_{\rm GW}}. } \]
  • \(|{\cal T}|\ll1\): instantaneous evolution is primarily GW-driven.
  • \(|{\cal T}|\sim1\): gas and GW radiation reaction are dynamically comparable.
  • \(|{\cal T}|\gg1\): gas-dominated migration; perturbative treatment may fail.

Importantly, \(|{\cal T}|\ll1\) does not guarantee that the gas is unobservable, because phase perturbations accumulate coherently.

Migration timescales

\[ t_{\rm gas} = \left| \frac{L}{T_{\rm gas}} \right|, \] \[ t_{\rm GW} = \left| \frac{L}{\dot L_{\rm GW}} \right|. \]

Then

\[ \frac{t_{\rm GW}}{t_{\rm gas}} = \left| \frac{T_{\rm gas}}{\dot L_{\rm GW}} \right|. \]

Save both \({\cal T}(r)\) and the integrated \(\delta\Phi\). The first provides the dynamical picture, while the second is closer to a gravitational-wave observable.

Two selection functions

Define

\[ p_{\rm det}(\lambda) \]

for detecting the binary, and

\[ p_{\rm env}(\lambda,{\cal D}_{\rm AGN}) \]

for measuring its environment. Then

\[ N_{\rm GW} = \int {\cal R}\,p_{\rm det}, \] \[ \boxed{ N_{\rm env} = \int {\cal R}\, p_{\rm det}\, p_{\rm env}. } \]

24. Research questions enabled by this framework

Q1. Which AGN-generated sources are actually observable by LISA?

Calculate

\[ p_{\rm det}(M_\bullet,\mu,z,e,a_\bullet). \]

Compare intrinsic and detected populations.

Q2. Does LISA strongly reshape the intrinsic mass-ratio distribution?

Compare

\[ p_{\rm intrinsic}(q) \]

with

\[ p_{\rm detected}(q). \]

Q3. Are hierarchical AGN merger products preferentially detected?

Larger secondaries usually produce stronger GW amplitudes but also change the inspiral timescale and waveform-modeling regime.

Q4. How many detected systems are IMRIs rather than stellar-mass EMRIs?

This connects hierarchical BH growth in AGN disks directly to an observable LISA population.

Q5. Which disk parameters produce measurable waveform deviations?

Map

\[ p_{\rm env} ( \Sigma,\, H/r,\, \alpha_{\rm visc},\, \dot M,\, q,\, M_\bullet ). \]

Q6. Can stochastic or time-dependent torques be distinguished from smooth migration?

Compare waveforms based on

\[ \langle T_{\rm gas}\rangle \]

with waveforms generated from the full

\[ T_{\rm gas}(t). \]

Q7. Do 2D and 3D hydrodynamic torque differences become measurable?

This directly connects numerical disk-systematics studies to a gravitational-wave observable.

Q8. Can vacuum-template searches lose SNR for strongly gas-perturbed sources?

Calculate

\[ {\rm FF},\qquad \rho_{\rm rec}. \]

Q9. Can environmental effects bias SMBH mass and spin inference?

Inject gas-perturbed waveforms and recover them with vacuum templates.

Q10. Can detected population properties distinguish AGN-disk formation from nuclear-cluster channels?

Useful discriminants may include

\[ q,\quad e,\quad \iota_{\rm Kerr},\quad a_\bullet,\quad T_{\rm gas}. \]

25. Recommended staged computational workflow

Stage 1 — Source screening

For every simulated binary calculate

\[ q,\quad M_{\bullet,z},\quad \mu_z,\quad D_L,\quad f_{\rm ISCO} \]

or a more appropriate Kerr strong-field frequency.

Stage 2 — Fast approximate SNR

Use a sky-averaged sensitivity and a simple circular inspiral model to identify obviously weak systems.

Stage 3 — Relativistic vacuum waveform

Generate an EMRI/IMRI waveform appropriate for the mass ratio and orbital state.

Stage 4 — Full LISA response

Compute

\[ h_+,h_\times \rightarrow h_A,h_E \rightarrow \rho. \]

Stage 5 — Orientation marginalization

Construct

\[ p_{\rm det}. \]

Stage 6 — Population convolution

Calculate

\[ N_{\rm det}. \]

Stage 7 — Inject hydrodynamic environment

Use

\[ T_{\rm gas}(r,t) \]

to modify orbital evolution.

Stage 8 — Environmental observability

Calculate

\[ \delta\Phi,\qquad {\rm FF},\qquad \rho_{\rm env}. \]

Stage 9 — Bayesian inference for selected systems

Infer parameters such as

\[ p( M_\bullet,\mu,a,e,\Sigma,\alpha_{\rm visc},\dot M,\ldots |d ). \]
This staged strategy avoids spending expensive waveform and Bayesian-analysis time on systems that are already clearly outside the scientifically useful LISA parameter space.

27. Major systematic uncertainties

Astrophysical population

  • AGN abundance and redshift evolution;
  • AGN duty cycle;
  • BH supply to the disk;
  • capture efficiency;
  • migration traps;
  • hierarchical merger efficiency;
  • AGN lifetime;
  • secondary BH accretion;
  • binary-single and binary-disk interactions.

Waveform model

Particularly important in the transition regime \(q\sim10^{-4}-10^{-2}\), where pure EMRI approximations may accumulate non-negligible systematic phase error.

LISA response and sensitivity

The selected response/noise model must be stated explicitly. Galactic binary confusion may contribute additional effective foreground noise in part of the millihertz band.

Hydrodynamic torque modeling

Disk torques can depend strongly on dimensionality, numerical resolution, thermodynamics, disk thickness, viscosity, and treatment of the secondary's vicinity.

Environmental waveform model

A Newtonian migration prescription may become inadequate deep in the SMBH potential. Relativistic consistency becomes increasingly important as the orbit approaches the strong-field regime.

Detection threshold

Present results for more than one \(\rho_{\rm th}\) to show sensitivity to search assumptions.

28. Validation tests

1. Distance scaling

Verify numerically that

\[ \rho\propto D_L^{-1} \]

for otherwise identical systems.

2. Observation-time scaling

For a slowly evolving test signal verify approximately

\[ \rho\propto T_{\rm obs}^{1/2}. \]

3. FFT normalization

Confirm that time-domain and frequency-domain implementations of the inner product are consistent.

4. Sky averaging

Compare Monte-Carlo averaged full-response SNR with the result from a sky-averaged effective sensitivity.

5. Zero-gas limit

Require

\[ T_{\rm gas}\rightarrow0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad h_{\rm gas}\rightarrow h_{\rm vac}. \]

6. Perturbative torque scaling

For sufficiently small torque verify approximately

\[ \delta\Phi\propto T_{\rm gas}. \]

7. Hydrodynamic convergence

Check dependence on grid resolution, domain size, softening, sink treatment, and 2D versus 3D modeling.

8. Waveform-model comparison

For the largest mass ratios in the population, compare more than one waveform approximation.

\[ {\cal R}_{\rm AGN} ( M_\bullet,\mu,e,a_\bullet, {\cal D}_{\rm disk},z ). \]

LISA observes

\[ \boxed{ {\cal R}_{\rm detected} = {\cal R}_{\rm AGN} \times p_{\rm det}. } \]

The selection function is fundamentally determined by

\[ \boxed{ \rho^2 = 4 \sum_C \int df\, \frac{ |\tilde h_C(f)|^2 }{ S_C(f) }. } \]

For environmental science, add a second selection:

\[ \boxed{ {\cal R}_{\rm env} = {\cal R}_{\rm AGN} \times p_{\rm det} \times p_{\rm env}. } \]

The gas information enters dynamically through

\[ \boxed{ \dot L = \dot L_{\rm GW} + T_{\rm gas}, } \]

and observationally through the waveform difference

\[ \boxed{ h_{\rm AGN}-h_{\rm vac,best}. } \]
A particularly strong AGN-LISA project therefore connects hydrodynamically predicted torques and populations to a LISA selection function and then asks which disk properties survive parameter degeneracies strongly enough to become measurable gravitational-wave observables.
